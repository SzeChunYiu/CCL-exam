Audio Pace & Voice Calibration

The supplied Cantonese CCL practice recordings were used as a pacing and delivery benchmark. Source speech was measured separately from the chime and inter-segment silence.

Measured reference pace

  • English median: about 160 words per minute; most measured segments were roughly 141–196 wpm.
  • Cantonese: about 4.0 Chinese characters per second; most measured segments were roughly 3.5–4.4 characters/second.
  • Real speakers vary naturally: short reactions may be quick, while condition-heavy sentences contain small pauses and emphasis.

V3 audio

V3 uses prerecorded Microsoft neural synthetic voices stored as individual MP3 segments in Supabase Storage. It alternates Australian-English voices William / Natasha and Hong Kong Cantonese voices WanLung / HiuGaai by speaker role.

The target synthesis rate is normal speed for Australian English and a small +5% adjustment for Cantonese, chosen to move the generated material toward the measured sample pace without flattening every segment into one mechanical duration.

Playback

Mock Exam mode uses 1.0×. Training modes can use 0.9×, 1.0× or 1.08×. The two-tone interpretation cue is generated after each neural source segment. Legacy bundled audio and a suitable device voice are fallback paths only.

Important: these are synthetic practice voices, not official NAATI recordings or human actors. Matching the reference pace is intended to improve practice realism, not to predict the exact speed of a future live test.
